Our Story

Why we started

Unbound Academics started with three people who had all been the student who was underestimated. All three of us went on to graduate college, two of us on full scholarships and as the first in our families to get there. None of us made it on the first method anyone handed us.

Ethan spent most of high school being told he wasn't applying himself. What was actually happening was ADHD, undiagnosed and unaccounted for. Once he found systems that worked with how he processed information instead of against it, everything changed. He graduated Summa Cum Laude from the University of Houston and is now a graduate student at Rice.

Lam knew the material cold. He could do the homework, explain the concepts, and walk anyone else through the problems. Then the test would hit his desk and it would all go quiet. Test anxiety is one of the most common reasons capable students underperform and one of the least often addressed, because tutoring tends to assume the gap is knowledge when the gap is pressure. What he needed wasn't another review session. It was a way to hold onto what he already knew when it counted. Once he had that, the scores followed, and they carried him to college on a full ride as the first in his family to go.

Toby was the kid who never needed help in math. He was strong enough there that everyone assumed he was fine everywhere, so nobody looked closely at language arts. The gap held until SAT prep, where reading and grammar are half the score and there is nowhere to hide. He got tutored, learned the skills nobody had ever actually taught him, and the score he earned took him to college on a full ride, also the first in his family. Those points were always available to him. He just needed someone to show him where they were.

Three students, three different things standing in the way, and not one of them was a lack of ability. That is the whole reason we started this. A student who can't focus, a student who freezes on tests, and a student with one specific blind spot need three different plans, but most tutoring hands all three the same script and hopes it fits.

So we don't. We figure out what is actually in the way first, match students with tutors who fit them, and align the work to Texas TEKS standards so it reinforces what happens in the classroom.

We're unbound because a student's potential shouldn't be defined by the first method that failed them.
